GPU sizing mathematics

Updated 24 September 2026. Model-state calculations, architecture references and explicit sensitivity inputs. No GPU benchmark has been run. GB = 10⁹ bytes.

Change the assumptions

Device capacityRoom for activations after state/runtimeScenario fits policy?

A fit calculation does not establish throughput, accuracy or framework compatibility. Sensitivity inputs are editable assumptions, not measured activations. The 20% policy has already been applied once. Advertised GB are a conservative convention; qualify actual device-visible bytes. Two devices do not automatically pool VRAM.

What is inside the calculation?

Required capacity = (weights + gradients + optimizer + activations + runtime) / (1 − free fraction)

Selected mixed-precision Adam: 2-byte compute weights + 4-byte master weights + 4-byte gradients + 8-byte moments = 18 bytes/trainable parameter. Other layouts use 16 bytes. Frozen-base LoRA adds optimizer/gradient state only for its adapters. Read the memory accounting.

Classic encoder saved activations: L × S × B × H × (34 + 5 × heads × S / H) bytes. This published reference is not an exact equation for Qwen GQA/SwiGLU, vision encoders or DeBERTa relative attention.

The selected Qwen mixed export uses exact tensor payload bytes read from all seven safetensors headers; the additional NF4 and FP32-upcast cases are explicitly derived alternatives. PEFT preparation can change tensor dtypes; this remains a tensor-placement baseline, not an exact runtime allocation. Quantization state and dequantization require additional space.

VRAM is different from total training work

Processed positions = examples × epochs × mean processed sequence length

Optimizer steps = epochs × ceil(examples / (microbatch × accumulation × data-parallel devices))

For dense linear layers, forward computation is approximately 2PT FLOPs. Full training adds input and weight gradients: approximately 6PT. Frozen-base adapter training is approximately 4PbaseT + 6PadapterT. These are leading-order calculations, excluding attention, vision, checkpoint recomputation and quantization costs; scaling-law compute accounting provides the full-training approximation. Frozen-base coefficient is our operation-count derivation.

Nominal 32B adapter job at 6,251 training examples × 3 epochs × 2,048 positions: T=38.406144M; base linear work≈4 × 32B × 38.406144M=4.916×10¹⁸ FLOPs. That is not GPU-hours. Hours = total FLOPs / (measured effective FLOP/s × 3,600). Vendor peak FLOP/s cannot be substituted for workload throughput without an explicit efficiency assumption.

Additional GPUs are needed for concurrent independent jobs, continuous serving, a validated completion deadline, or actual model-state sharding. DDP replicates weights; ZeRO partitions selected state and has communication/gather overhead.
